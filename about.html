About

Bridging spectroscopy and simulation

I am a PhD candidate in Physical Chemistry at the University of Campinas (UNICAMP), working under the supervision of Prof. Dr. Cláudio F. Tormena and co-supervised by Prof. Dr. Matthias Stein (Max Planck Institute Magdeburg).

Biography

My research sits at the intersection of Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, molecular dynamics, and quantum mechanical calculations. I am particularly interested in how chiral molecules interact with their environment and with chiral resolving agents—and how these interactions can be exploited to differentiate enantiomers by diffusion NMR (DOSY).

I completed my BSc in Chemistry (Licenciatura) at the Universidade Federal Fluminense (UFF) in 2021, graduating with a coefficient of 9.1 and receiving the Láurea Acadêmica. During my undergraduate studies, I was a PIBIC/CNPq fellow working on computational chemistry of 2D materials, which sparked my interest in combining computational and experimental approaches.

In 2021, I started my Direct PhD at UNICAMP with a FAPESP scholarship. From 2023 to 2025, I was a visiting researcher at the Max Planck Institute for Dynamics of Complex Technical Systems in Magdeburg, Germany, supported by a BEPE-FAPESP fellowship and later by a Max Planck Society partial PhD scholarship. There, I worked on integrating NMR experiments with multiscale simulations for chiral recognition.

Outside the lab, I enjoy developing open-source tools for data analysis, teaching, and scientific communication.

Further information on me

Teaching & Outreach

Sharing science, inside and outside the classroom. From undergraduate laboratory courses to public engagement projects, teaching has been a constant thread throughout my academic journey.

I believe that the best science communicators are those who never lose their sense of curiosity. My goal as a teacher is to help students build intuition—about spectroscopy, about computation, and about how to think like a scientist—rather than simply memorizing procedures.

I've had the privilege of teaching across a wide range of formats: undergraduate lab courses, flipped-classroom projects, science outreach for high-school students, and one-on-one mentoring of junior researchers.
